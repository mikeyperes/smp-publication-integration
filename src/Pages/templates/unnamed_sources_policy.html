[hws_site field="name"] names its sources whenever it can, because readers should be able to judge where information comes from. We grant anonymity only as a last resort.

When we use an unnamed source

  • The information is important to the story and of real public interest.
  • It cannot be obtained any other way, on the record.
  • The source could face real harm, such as losing their job or their safety, if named.
  • The source is in a position to know the information firsthand.

How we handle it

  • At least one editor knows who the source is and approves its use.
  • We confirm the information with a second source or a document wherever we can.
  • We tell readers why the source was not named and what puts them in a position to know, for example "a staff member who was not authorized to speak publicly".
  • We do not allow unnamed sources to make personal attacks or offer opinions.
  • Once we promise anonymity, we keep that promise.
